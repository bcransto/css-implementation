# Teacher interview: what you do now

One sheet per teacher, ELA or math. The numbered text is the script, written to be read aloud to the teacher. *[Bracketed italics are notes for the interviewer: prompts, background, and the database field each answer fills. Don't read them aloud.]* Answers go into that teacher's *observed* rows on the area page (basis = observed, status = unwritten). Terms in bold are NCII vocabulary; the plain gloss follows.

*[Before you start, write down: teacher's name, grade and subject, interview date. Fills: area; instructor.]*

## Area and core
1. Does your grade have a FLEX block, and do you staff it? What's the **core instruction** in your grade: the program everyone gets, or does it depend on the section? What does your core block actually look like, beyond the program's name?

   *[Fills: has_flex; flex_duty; core; Tier 1 instruction.]*
2. **Training**: which programs or instruments have you been trained on, and when — Just Words, Read Naturally, Bridges, DIBELS, STAR, Wilson, anything? Who else in the building is trained on them, that you know of?

   *[This is the "trained para" question. Fills: instructor_tool.]*

## Universal screening and the flag (Tier 1)
3. What do you use as a **screener** in September to decide who's at risk: MCAS, STAR, ALEKS, a reading inventory, last year's teacher, a pre-test of your own? If it's DIBELS or STAR, which **measure** do you actually read: ORF, Maze, the composite, the scaled score?

   *[Fills: screening table; measure.]*
4. Is there a **cut score**, even an informal one, like "below 40% on ALEKS" or "Partially Meeting", or is it judgment?

   *[Fills: cut; Tier 1 intervention entry rule.]*
5. Is there anything you give only to some kids, rather than to everyone — a level check for a kid you're unsure about, a probe on request, a diagnostic a specialist runs?

   *[Fills: screening table, role level_check / on_request.]*
6. After the September screen, is there any second look before a kid goes into intervention, such as a few weeks of watching, a probe or two, a quiz, or a different, longer test given one-to-one, before deciding the flag is real? Or does the screen alone decide?

   *[**Multi-gated screening**, also called second-stage screening: Fuchs recommends a second look after the initial screen to cut false positives. Either works: a different, individually given test, or six to eight weeks of progress monitoring every two weeks. Note which one they describe. Fills: Tier 1 PM tool; PM measure; PM rule.]*
7. While a kid is flagged but not yet in a group, what do they get in class: reteaching, an in-class small group, a seat change, nothing different?

   *[Fills: Tier 1 supports.]*
8. What takes the flag off a kid who never goes to a group: a later screen, a few weeks of looking fine, a unit test, it just fades? And separately, what tips a kid from flagged into a Tier 2 group — a decision, a date, a second score, or is the kid in the group from day one?

   *[Fills: flag removed when; Tier 2 entry when.]*
9. Are there later **screening windows**: a mid-year one, such as January, and an end-of-year one, such as May or June? Is the September call the call, or does a kid get re-screened and picked up, or released, later? Does the end-of-year screen feed anything, such as next year's placement or the handoff?

   *[Fills: windows.]*

## Sorting (diagnostic profiles)
10. When a kid is at risk, do you distinguish kinds of need, or is it one bucket?

    *[Their words for the categories are what we want. Don't offer these unless they're stuck. Math: facts, fractions, equations, word problems, "doesn't do the work". Reading: decoding, fluency, comprehension only, vocabulary, "doesn't read".]*

    Is there any **diagnostic assessment** behind the sorting, or is it from class work?

    *[Fills: profiles; sort rule.]*
11. When you put a flagged kid into one of those groups, what decides it: a unit assessment, a STAR domain report, a fact-fluency check, a running record, class work, your judgment?

    *[Fills: placement tool.]*
12. Are the kids in a group there for the same reason, or is a group whoever's flagged that period? If it's mixed, does the instruction differ by kid?

    *[Fills: profiles; group size; status note.]*

## The intervention (Tier 2)
13. What **intervention** does a flagged kid get: FLEX group, in-class small group, ALEKS time, after-school, nothing formal?

    *[Fills: dosage; Tier 2 intervention entry rule.]*
14. How many weeks pass between the September screen and the group actually starting? What happens in that gap?

    *[Fills: Tier 1 PM rule; status note.]*
15. Who delivers it: you, another teacher, a para?

    *[Fills: delivered by.]*
16. **Intensity**: how often, how long is each session, and roughly what group size?

    *[Fills: dosage; group size.]*
17. What does a session actually contain: a program lesson, fact practice, rereading, homework help, a mix?

    *[Fills: Tier 2 instruction.]*
18. Is there an **intervention program** or set of materials, or is it your own? If it's a program, is it one with evidence behind it, that you know of?

    *[Fills: program.]*
19. Does anyone besides you work with these kids on this subject, such as a reading specialist, interventionist or SLP?

    *[Fills: delivered by.]*
20. Do you use any published program or materials at all, such as Just Words, Read Naturally, Bridges, even alongside your own?

    *[Fills: program.]*
21. Are any of the flagged kids on an IEP where sped is really running the intervention? Do you treat them differently, and does their data reach you?

    *[Fills: status: iep; delivered by.]*

## Progress monitoring and decision rules
22. What **progress monitoring tool** do you use to see whether it's working: a PM sheet, STAR or ALEKS progress, unit tests, a probe, gut? If it's DIBELS or STAR, which **measure**: ORF, Maze, the scaled score?

    *[Fills: PM tool; PM measure.]*
23. What number do you actually look at on it: problems correct per minute, words correct per minute, percent on the unit test, ALEKS topics mastered, a STAR scaled score, a level? Is that check the same kind of test at the same difficulty all year, or does it change as the unit changes? Do you chart the scores and look at the trend, or go by the latest one?

    *[**General outcome measure** (same test all year, so September and March scores compare) or **mastery measure** (a new test for each skill or unit). Unit tests and ALEKS topics mastered are mastery measures. Write down which, in the PM metric. Fills: PM metric.]*
24. How often do you monitor, and is there a **decision rule**, meaning a point where the data tells you to change something, like "if nothing moves in four weeks I switch"? When it does say change something, how do you decide what to change: a **diagnostic assessment**, a look at the kid's errors, the next thing in the program, a guess?

    *[A diagnostic here means a test of which skills the kid has and lacks, given when progress stalls; not a disability diagnosis. Fills: PM rule.]*
25. Is there a scheduled review, and does anyone else look at the data?

    *[Fills: review.]*
26. Can I see it: a PM sheet, a gradebook export, an ALEKS or STAR report, anything written? Or is this from memory?

    *[Fills: evidence — PM sheet, interview, self-report.]*
27. **Record keeping**: where does the record of a flagged kid live — a PM sheet, a gradebook, a binder, a shared drive, your head? Does anyone else have access to it? If you left the school in June, what would the next teacher find about these kids, and what would walk out the door with you?

    *[Fills: evidence; status note.]*

## Duration and exit criteria
28. How long does a kid stay in: a fixed number of weeks, a term, "until they're better", or indefinitely?

    *[Fills: weeks.]*
29. Has a kid ever formally exited? What were the **exit criteria**, even unwritten ones?

    *[Fills: exit rule.]*
30. What happens when it works: back to class only, stays in the group, something else?

    *[Several teachers have said: "Tier 2 simply stops; the kid was in Tier 1 the whole time." Fills: on response.]*
31. **Non-response**: what happens to the kid who doesn't improve? CST, sped referral, stays in the group, nothing?

    *[Fills: on non-response.]*

## Tier 3
32. Is there anything more intensive than the Tier 2 group — one-to-one, a specialist, a different program, more minutes — for a kid who hasn't responded? Who is in it now, how did they get there, and is it a district rule, an IEP, or something you arranged? How often is that kid's progress checked, and does the instruction get adjusted for that one kid based on it?

    *[NCII's **data-based individualization** expects weekly progress monitoring at this level. Fills: Tier 3 row; Tier 3 PM rule; status.]*

## Subject specifics
33. *[Math teachers: the pre-algebra/proportional profile.]* Do you see the kid who's fine on arithmetic but lost on equations, and what do you do with them?

    *[Reading teachers.]* Do you see the kid who decodes fine but can't comprehend, and does that kid go anywhere, such as the SLP?

    *[Fills: status note.]*
34. **Handoff**: what goes with a struggling kid to next year's teacher in June — a list, a conversation, the PM sheet, nothing?

    *[Grade 8: ask the same question about the high school. Fills: handoff route; status note.]*

## Your read
35. What would you want, if you could have one thing?

    *[Fills: status note.]*

## Where the answers land on the page
