# Teacher interview: what you do now

One sheet per teacher, ELA or math. Answers go into that teacher's *observed* rows on the area page (basis = observed, status = unwritten). Terms in bold are NCII vocabulary; the plain gloss follows.

## Area and core
1. Teacher's name, grade and subject, interview date. What's the **core instruction** in this grade: the program everyone gets, or does it depend on the section?

## Universal screening and the flag (Tier 1)
2. What does she use as a **screener** in September to decide who's at risk: MCAS, STAR, ALEKS, a reading inventory, last year's teacher, a pre-test of her own?
3. Is there a **cut score**, even an informal one ("below 40% on ALEKS", "Partially Meeting"), or is it judgment?
4. After the September screen, is there any second look before a kid goes into intervention, such as a few weeks of watching, a probe or two, a quiz, before deciding the flag is real? Or does the screen alone decide? (**Multi-gated screening**: Fuchs recommends a short monitoring stage after the initial screen to cut false positives.)
5. Is there a second **screening window** later in the year, such as January, or is the September call the call?

## Sorting (diagnostic profiles)
6. When a kid is at risk, does she distinguish kinds of need, or is it one bucket? Her words for the categories are what we want. Math: facts, fractions, equations, word problems, "doesn't do the work". Reading: decoding, fluency, comprehension only, vocabulary, "doesn't read". Is there any **diagnostic assessment** behind the sorting, or is it from class work?

## The intervention (Tier 2)
7. What **intervention** does a flagged kid get: FLEX group, in-class small group, ALEKS time, after-school, nothing formal?
8. Who delivers it: her, another teacher, a para?
9. **Intensity**: how often, how long each session, and roughly what group size?
10. Is there an **intervention program** or set of materials, or is it her own? If a program, is it one with evidence behind it that she knows of?
11. Does anyone besides her work with these kids on this subject, such as a reading specialist, interventionist or SLP?
12. Does she use any published program or materials at all, such as Just Words, Read Naturally, Bridges, even alongside her own?

## Progress monitoring and decision rules
13. What **progress monitoring tool** does she use to see whether it's working: a PM sheet, STAR or ALEKS progress, unit tests, a probe, gut?
14. How often does she monitor, and is there a **decision rule**, meaning a point where the data tells her to change something ("if nothing moves in four weeks I switch")?
15. Is there a scheduled review, and does anyone else look at the data?

## Duration and exit criteria
16. How long does a kid stay in: a fixed number of weeks, a term, "until they're better", or indefinitely?
17. Has a kid ever formally exited? What were the **exit criteria**, even unwritten ones?
18. What happens when it works: back to class only, stays in the group, something else? (Several teachers: "Tier 2 simply stops; the kid was in Tier 1 the whole time.")
19. **Non-response**: what happens to the kid who doesn't improve? CST, sped referral, stays in the group, nothing?

## Subject specifics
20. Math: the pre-algebra/proportional profile. Does she see the kid who's fine on arithmetic but lost on equations, and what does she do with them?
    Reading: the kid who decodes fine but can't comprehend. Does she see that profile, and does it go anywhere, such as the SLP?
21. Grade 8 only: what does she hand to the high school in June about a struggling kid, if anything?

## Her read
22. What would she want, if she could have one thing?

## Where the answers land on the page
2 to 5: the screening table and the Tier 1 intervention entry rule and PM rule. 6: profiles. 7 to 12: program, delivered by, dosage, group size, and the row's status note. 13 to 15: PM tool, PM metric, PM rule, review. 16 to 19: weeks, exit rule, on response, on non-response. 20 to 22: status note.
