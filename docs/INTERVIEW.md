# Teacher interview: what you do now

One sheet per teacher, ELA or math. Answers go into that teacher's *observed* rows on the area page (basis = observed, status = unwritten). Terms in bold are NCII vocabulary; the plain gloss follows. The database field each answer fills is in parentheses.

## Area and core
1. Teacher's name, grade and subject, interview date (area; instructor). Does the grade have a FLEX block, and does he staff it (has_flex; flex_duty)? What's the **core instruction** in this grade: the program everyone gets, or does it depend on the section (core)? What does the core block actually look like beyond the program's name (Tier 1 instruction)?
2. **Training**: which programs or instruments has he been trained on, and when — Just Words, Read Naturally, Bridges, DIBELS, STAR, Wilson, anything? Who else in the building is trained on them, that he knows of? (instructor_tool; the "trained para" question)

## Universal screening and the flag (Tier 1)
3. What does he use as a **screener** in September to decide who's at risk: MCAS, STAR, ALEKS, a reading inventory, last year's teacher, a pre-test of his own (screening table)? If it's DIBELS or STAR, which **measure** does he actually read: ORF, Maze, the composite, the scaled score (measure)?
4. Is there a **cut score**, even an informal one ("below 40% on ALEKS", "Partially Meeting"), or is it judgment (cut; Tier 1 intervention entry rule)?
5. Is there anything he gives only to some kids — a level check for a kid he's unsure about, a probe on request, a diagnostic a specialist runs — rather than to everyone (screening table: role level_check / on_request)?
6. After the September screen, is there any second look before a kid goes into intervention, such as a few weeks of watching, a probe or two, a quiz, before deciding the flag is real? Or does the screen alone decide? (**Multi-gated screening**: Fuchs recommends a short monitoring stage after the initial screen to cut false positives.) (Tier 1 PM tool; PM rule)
7. While a kid is flagged but not yet in a group, what does he get in class: reteaching, an in-class small group, a seat change, nothing different (Tier 1 supports)?
8. What takes the flag off a kid who never goes to a group: a later screen, a few weeks of looking fine, a unit test, it just fades (flag removed when)? And separately, what tips a kid from flagged into a Tier 2 group — a decision, a date, a second score, or is the kid in the group from day one (Tier 2 entry when)?
9. Are there later **screening windows**: a mid-year one, such as January, and an end-of-year one, such as May or June? Is the September call the call, or does a kid get re-screened and picked up (or released) later? Does the end-of-year screen feed anything, such as next year's placement or the handoff (windows)?

## Sorting (diagnostic profiles)
10. When a kid is at risk, does he distinguish kinds of need, or is it one bucket? His words for the categories are what we want. Math: facts, fractions, equations, word problems, "doesn't do the work". Reading: decoding, fluency, comprehension only, vocabulary, "doesn't read". Is there any **diagnostic assessment** behind the sorting, or is it from class work (profiles; sort rule)?
11. When he puts a flagged kid into one of those groups, what decides it: a unit assessment, a STAR domain report, a fact-fluency check, a running record, class work, his judgment (placement tool)?
12. Are the kids in a group there for the same reason, or is a group whoever's flagged that period? If mixed, does the instruction differ by kid (profiles; group size; status note)?

## The intervention (Tier 2)
13. What **intervention** does a flagged kid get: FLEX group, in-class small group, ALEKS time, after-school, nothing formal (dosage; Tier 2 intervention entry rule)?
14. How many weeks pass between the September screen and the group actually starting? What happens in that gap (Tier 1 PM rule; status note)?
15. Who delivers it: him, another teacher, a para (delivered by)?
16. **Intensity**: how often, how long each session, and roughly what group size (dosage; group size)?
17. What does a session actually contain: a program lesson, fact practice, rereading, homework help, a mix (Tier 2 instruction)?
18. Is there an **intervention program** or set of materials, or is it his own? If a program, is it one with evidence behind it that he knows of (program)?
19. Does anyone besides him work with these kids on this subject, such as a reading specialist, interventionist or SLP (delivered by)?
20. Does he use any published program or materials at all, such as Just Words, Read Naturally, Bridges, even alongside his own (program)?
21. Are any of the flagged kids on an IEP where sped is really running the intervention? Does he treat them differently, and does their data reach him (status: iep; delivered by)?

## Progress monitoring and decision rules
22. What **progress monitoring tool** does he use to see whether it's working: a PM sheet, STAR or ALEKS progress, unit tests, a probe, gut (PM tool)?
23. What number does he actually look at on it: problems correct per minute, words correct per minute, percent on the unit test, ALEKS topics mastered, a STAR scaled score, a level (PM metric)?
24. How often does he monitor, and is there a **decision rule**, meaning a point where the data tells him to change something ("if nothing moves in four weeks I switch") (PM rule)?
25. Is there a scheduled review, and does anyone else look at the data (review)?
26. Can I see it: a PM sheet, a gradebook export, an ALEKS or STAR report, anything written? Or is this from memory (evidence: PM sheet, interview, self-report)?
27. **Record keeping**: where does the record of a flagged kid live — a PM sheet, a gradebook, a binder, a shared drive, his head? Does anyone else have access to it? If he left the school in June, what would the next teacher find about these kids, and what would walk out the door with him (evidence; status note)?

## Duration and exit criteria
28. How long does a kid stay in: a fixed number of weeks, a term, "until they're better", or indefinitely (weeks)?
29. Has a kid ever formally exited? What were the **exit criteria**, even unwritten ones (exit rule)?
30. What happens when it works: back to class only, stays in the group, something else? (Several teachers: "Tier 2 simply stops; the kid was in Tier 1 the whole time.") (on response)
31. **Non-response**: what happens to the kid who doesn't improve? CST, sped referral, stays in the group, nothing (on non-response)?

## Tier 3
32. Is there anything more intensive than the Tier 2 group — one-to-one, a specialist, a different program, more minutes — for a kid who hasn't responded? Who is in it now, how did they get there, and is it a district rule, an IEP, or something he arranged (Tier 3 row; status)?

## Subject specifics
33. Math: the pre-algebra/proportional profile. Does he see the kid who's fine on arithmetic but lost on equations, and what does he do with them?
    Reading: the kid who decodes fine but can't comprehend. Does he see that profile, and does it go anywhere, such as the SLP? (status note)
34. **Handoff**: what goes with a struggling kid to next year's teacher in June — a list, a conversation, the PM sheet, nothing? Grade 8: same question for the high school (handoff route; status note).

## His read
35. What would he want, if he could have one thing (status note)?

## Where the answers land on the page
1: area, core, the instructor row, Tier 1 instruction. 2: the instructor's training rows. 3 to 9: the screening table and the Tier 1 block (intervention entry rule, further screening, Tier 1 supports, flag removed when, Tier 2 entry when). 10 to 12: profiles, sort rule, placement tool. 13 to 21: program, instruction, delivered by, dosage, group size, Tier 2 intervention entry rule, and the row's status note. 22 to 27: PM tool, PM metric, PM rule, review, evidence, and the record-keeping note. 28 to 31: weeks, exit rule, on response, on non-response. 32: the Tier 3 row. 33 to 35: status note and handoff route.
